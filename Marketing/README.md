# Reef Run — Steam screenshot workflow

Six gameplay screenshots for the Steam store page, captured from the real game systems in a dedicated
photo-mode scene. No marketing text, logos or UI is baked in.

**Primary store screenshot: 01.** It reads in under a second, even as a thumbnail: one big, clear silhouette with an
open mouth against bright water. It states the premise ("one of you is the shark"), and the fleeing school
hints at the prey side. The open space in front of the shark carries the motion.

Steam (checked against partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/standard on 2026-10-16) requires at least 5
screenshots at 1920×1080 minimum, 16:9, gameplay only, and at least 4 flagged "suitable for all ages". **Flag
01, 03, 04, 05 and 06 as all-ages; 02 shows blood.** Capsule art is a separate job (main 1232×706, header
920×430, small 462×174, vertical 748×896); these screenshots are not capsules.

## How to use it

- **Tools > Marketing > Open Showcase Scene** opens `Assets/Scenes/MarketingShowcase.unity`.
- **Tools > Marketing > Capture All Shots** enters Play mode, waits for the reef to build, and writes every shot
  to `Marketing/Screenshots/NN_slug.png`. **Capture Current Shot** (Ctrl+Alt+K) captures the selected shot.
- **Next / Previous Shot** (Ctrl+Alt+] / [) step through shots in Play mode. Each one sets up and freezes.
  **Resume Time** lets it run again.
- **Build Contact Sheet** writes `Marketing/ContactSheet.png`.
- Each shot is a child of *Marketing Showcase*. Its transform is the camera: use **Set From Scene View** /
  **Move Scene View To Shot** in its inspector. Its fields cover FOV, grade (shark or fish eyes), depth of field,
  exposure/contrast/saturation, chromatic aberration, sun, settle time, seed, world event, and objects to hide.
  Child `ShowcaseActor` marks place the shark, player fish, schools and turtles. Child lights are on only for
  that shot (01 has a rim light).
- Resolution and supersampling live on the *Marketing Showcase* component (default 1920×1080, 2×).

## How it stays honest

Everything on screen is the game's own code and assets:
- the menu shark model, player-size fish, BoidsManager schools that flee the shark through `Predator`;
- `Edible.Devour` for the catch (blood, crunch, the mouth anchor on the jaw bone);
- `SharkEatFx` / `SwimTrail` / `WaterFx`;
- the real `TangledTurtle` task prefab, placed on the spot the turtle task itself picked in a normal run;
- the FishAllies teammate glow material;
- world events through `MatchManager` / `WorldEvents`, run offline;
- `CreatureVision` for the role grades.

The camera renders exactly like the player camera (settings copied from the PlayerShark prefab). Capture renders a
separate camera into a texture, so there is no UI, gizmos or cursor.

## Files

Created:
- `Assets/Scenes/MarketingShowcase.unity`: a copy of IslandTest without the network players, HUD canvases and
  randomly placed task props, with the island-wide school switched off, plus the director, camera and 6 shots.
- `Assets/Scripts/Marketing/MarketingShowcase.cs`: director (shot setup, actors, events, grade, capture).
- `Assets/Scripts/Marketing/ShowcaseShot.cs`, `ShowcaseActor.cs`: shot and actor-mark components.

  contact sheet.
- `Marketing/Screenshots/*.png`, `Marketing/ContactSheet.png`, this README.

No production scene, prefab, material or project setting was changed. The game's own scripts are unchanged too.

## Known limitations

- **The reef isn't identical between Editor sessions.** The seabed is regenerated by GPU erosion every run (by
  design; see TerrainNetSync), and coral is placed by height, so scattered coral and kelp near a camera can move.
  Within one Play session a shot is reproducible: two Capture All runs gave the same composition (same school
  shape, same poses). Only time-driven detail (caustics, kelp sway, the turtle's struggle) differed. After
  reopening Unity, recheck the contact sheet and nudge a shot if a coral now blocks it.
- Nightfall's glowing plankton trails only come from live `FishPlayer` bodies, so a nightfall shot can't show
  them here. The jellyfish swarm is too sparse to read in one frame. Hence the trawler for variety.
- Looking west toward the arena edge (around x −150), faint vertical seams show in the water. They aren't caused
  by any scene object (the toggle tests ruled those out). No final shot looks that way.
- Shots 03 to 06 show the in-game teal teammate glow. On-screen UI (HUD, target brackets, sonar markers) isn't
  captured, by design.
