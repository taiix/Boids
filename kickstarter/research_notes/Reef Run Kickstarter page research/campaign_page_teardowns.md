# Campaign page teardowns: funded (and failed) multiplayer video-game Kickstarters, 2025–2026

Researched 2026-11-16.

**Method and limits (read first).**
- Each page below was opened in a browser at its `/description` URL and its story was walked in DOM order (headings, paragraph openings, images, GIFs, image-links). Funding figures come from the page sidebar and from the project's own dataset `kickstarter/research/ks_videogames_*.json` (Kickstarter Discover listing, pulled 2026-10-20).
- **Section order is exact. Heading wording is exact only where the heading is real text.** Many pages use banner *images* as section headers; those appear below as "(image header)" and their wording was not read. Fat Goblins and Carnival Hunt are almost entirely image-headed.
- **GIF counts are a lower bound** (counted by `.gif` source or Kickstarter's lazy-GIF class; WEBP animations and embedded videos are not counted). Image counts include header banners and reward graphics.
- The project **video** sits above the story and was not inspected. "First thing under the video" means the first story element.
- Quotes are short verbatim fragments from the page text as rendered on 2026-11-16. Pages of ended campaigns can be edited after the fact (several carry "funded" banners added mid-campaign).
- Not read this session: Hide Or Die, Mech Tech, Lou's Lagoon, All Will Rise, Avaria, Kioku, Tomo Endless Blue, Ahoy (already covered in `14_WHAT_SUCCESSFUL_CAMPAIGNS_DO.md`; their slugs were not re-fetched). Tunnel Story turned out to be a board game and is excluded.

## What is the exact section order and heading wording on each page? What is the first thing under the video?

### Takeaway
Funded pages in this niche open with a welcome/"What is X?" block of one banner, one or two short paragraphs and a GIF, then put Steam and Discord image-buttons within the first screen or two; rewards and stretch goals sit in the middle, and budget, team and "Why Kickstarter?" come last. Sections are short: most are a header banner, one image or GIF, and one to three sentences.

### Cited Findings

**Springfield Hotel** (33 images, at least 6 GIFs, about 750 words of story text) — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/description)
- Text headings in order: "Welcome" → "What is Springfield Hotel?" → "The Hotel is Always Listening" → "Core Gameplay Elements" → "Will You Solve The Mystery In Time?" → "The Doors Open...But Only For A Few" → "A Love Letter To Haunted Glamour" → "Reward Tiers" → "Kickstarter Exclusives" → "Quality Assurance" → "Diverse Character Design" → "Intricate Task Design" → "Multiplayer Game With Chapters" → "Streamer & Party Friendly" → "A Focus on Replayability" → "Did We Mention..." → "No AI in Game Creation" → "Stretch Goals" → (Steam and Discord image-buttons) → "How We Plan To Use Your Funding" → "Team Story" → "Our Partners".
- First thing under the video: heading "Welcome" followed by one image and one GIF, with no body text. Most sections are a heading plus a single graphic that carries the copy; only about 750 words on the page are live text.
- The hook section is "The Hotel is Always Listening": "Traditionally, social deduction games like Mafia or Werewolf assign fixed roles. In Springfield Hotel, your role is shaped by what you find." and "The louder you are, the faster the Ghoul…".
- GIFs carry joke captions, e.g. "Therapist: Try to release stress calmly. Me: 💥".
- Rewards appear at position 8 of 21, before the deeper feature sections; stretch goals, budget and team are the last four sections.

- Every section starts with a banner image, then one to three paragraphs. Four feature blurbs follow a row of three images, e.g. "Easy to pick up, impossible to put down. Perfect for parties, streams, and late-night hangouts."
- It names its comparables outright: "The perfect blend of games like Mario Party, Party Animals, Moving Out, and Nintendoland."

- Order: three images → platform/Early Access sentence with a Steam text link → open-playtest announcement with a Steam image-button → "The Game" → feature paragraphs (one image each) → "Socials".

**Cleaning Raccrew** (13 images, 0 GIFs, about 820 words; live) — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/teambrije/cleaning-raccrew/description)
- Order: "Cleaning Raccrew!" → Steam image-button → pitch → "The Story!" → "Meet The Contestants!" → "Even More Characters!!" → "The One Stretch Goal!" → "Many more species!" → Steam image-button again (last element).

### Inferences
- A common order across the funded pages: (1) welcome/what-is-it + GIF, (2) Steam/Discord/demo buttons, (3) the hook mechanic, (4) features or modes as GIF + two-sentence pairs, (5) rewards, (6) stretch goals, (7) budget, (8) roadmap, (9) team, (10) closing CTA. Springfield Hotel and The Boo Croo, the two closest $10k-goal comparables, both put rewards before the long feature tail or the budget, and both end on team.
- Banner-image headers are the norm (Boo Croo, Springfield, Fat Goblins, Carnival Hunt, Bold Club, Elevator). A page with plain text headings and few images (Jousting Around, Mischief House) looks visibly less produced next to them.
- Word counts for funded small-goal pages cluster around 400–1,900. Reef Run's v2 copy target of roughly 1,500 words is in range; Fat Goblins shows a solo developer can fund on about 390 words if the rest is GIFs and a playable build.

### Gaps
- Wording of image-based headers (all of Fat Goblins, most of Carnival Hunt, parts of Springfield) was not read; it would need a visual pass or OCR.
- The project video and the page's top "blurb" line were not captured for any page.
- Exact GIF counts are unverified (lower bounds only); Elevator and Rescue Ops may use video embeds or WEBP that the count missed.
- Hide Or Die, Mech Tech and the other already-studied pages were not re-read for layout this session.

## How do they present the Steam wishlist call to action: banner, text link, how many times, placed where, what wording?

### Takeaway
The standard is a pair of clickable banner images (Steam + Discord) placed once near the top, usually right after the first pitch paragraph, and sometimes repeated at the very bottom. Explicit "wishlist" wording in text is rarer than expected; when a demo exists the banner says "Play the demo" instead and becomes the first element on the page.

### Cited Findings
- **Fat Goblins:** Steam image-button after the pitch and origin paragraphs, followed by the only explicit ask to non-backers found in the set: "Even if you don't plan on pledging, consider supporting the game by Wishlisting Fat Goblins on Steam and joining the Playtests!" Then "Also Join The Discord to hang out with other Fat Goblins fans and learn more about the game!" and a Discord image-button. Two Steam links and two Discord links in total; the Steam URL carries a `utm_source=kickstart…` tag — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/kuju/fat-goblins-online-hat-based-pvp-inspired-by-fat-princess/description)
- **Deadly Trick:** Steam image-button is the first element, with the caption "▲ ▲ Click the image above to visit Deadly Trick’s Steam page." and "Adding the game to your wishlist helps us tremendously." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/finalblow/deadly-trick-a-reasoning-based-social-deduction-game/description)
- **Carnival Hunt:** first element is the heading "Follow along:" with Steam and Discord image-buttons; the Discord button repeats beside the stretch-goal callout (three Discord links, one Steam link) — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/badraa/carnival-hunt/description)
- **The Bold Club:** first element is the heading "Follow Us" with Steam and Discord image-buttons — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/kayugames/the-bold-club-the-card-game-that-dares-you-to-cheat/description)
- **BLACKOUT: Zero Point** and **Combo Devils:** Steam and Discord image-buttons directly after the introduction paragraphs, once each — [BLACKOUT](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/2138076565/blackout-zero-point-tactical-descent-into-chaos/description), [Combo Devils](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/punkzilla/combo-devils/description)
- **Springfield Hotel:** Steam and Discord image-buttons appear once, late, after "Stretch Goals" and before the budget section (one Steam link, one Discord link) — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/description)
- **The Boo Croo:** Steam and Discord image-buttons are the last two elements on the page, under "Join the Croo!" (one each) — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/description)
- **Elevator:** the Steam button links to the *demo* app and is the first story element; the pitch paragraph then says "The demo is playable with up to 8 Steam friends, and contains 18 randomized playable floors", followed by a plain link list "Discord / Bluesky / Steam". Later: "we're more of a show than tell kind of team, so if you really want to see the quality we're aiming for Elevator, please try out our demo." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/pixeltailgames/elevator-1/description)
- **Rescue Ops: Wildfire:** "Our open playtest is now live on Steam!" with a Steam image-button near the top; four Steam links in total — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1556468747/rescue-ops-wildfire-a-co-op-firefighting-simulation-game/description)
- **Cleaning Raccrew:** Steam image-button directly under the title and again as the final element — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/teambrije/cleaning-raccrew/description)
- **Mini Bash:** no Steam link at all; the CTA is early access for backers ("Founders Play First", "Kickstarter backers get early access.") plus a closing "Follow us, join the Discord, and bring your f…" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/digitaldaylight/minibash/description)
- Failed pages use the same device: Redhowl opens with the heading "PLAY THE DEMO" and a Steam image-button, repeated after the pitch (three Steam links); American Cooking Simulator opens with "Play the demo!" and a Steam image-button; Blue Ridge Hunting has a "DEMO" section with "Click the button above to play the free demo on Steam!" — [Redhowl](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/benomstudio/redhowl/description), [American Cooking Simulator](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description), [Blue Ridge Hunting](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/jadecompany/blue-ridge-hunting-co-op-appalachia-cryptid-hunting/description)
- American Cooking Simulator turned wishlists into a public "Social Goals" counter: "Steam Wishlists: 3,441/4000 (559 left)" and "Demo Players: 3,852/2000 ALL GOALS REACHED!" — it still failed at 32% of goal — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description)
- A creator postmortem (r/IndieDev, April 2025, three funded games) argues a wishlist is a one-click action that may never convert and suggests holding the Steam page until Kickstarter traffic can be funnelled into it — [r/IndieDev mirror](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/IndieDev/comments/1jou1tl/things_i_learned_from_running_3_funded_games_on) (seen only as a search-result summary, not read in full).

### Inferences
- Placement splits two ways: "follow buttons first" (Carnival Hunt, Bold Club, Deadly Trick, Elevator, Cleaning Raccrew) and "buttons after the first pitch paragraph" (Fat Goblins, BLACKOUT, Combo Devils). Bottom-only placement (Boo Croo, Springfield) is the minority. A top pair plus a repeat at the bottom covers both.
- Typical repetition is one or two Steam links per page, not a banner after every section. Four is the most seen on a funded page (Rescue Ops).
- The buttons are always custom images in the game's art style, never Steam's stock widget (Kickstarter stories cannot embed it), and Steam and Discord are always shown as a pair.
- Fat Goblins' line aimed at people who will not pledge is worth copying nearly verbatim: it gives a visitor who is not ready to pay a free action that still helps.
- Because a demo/wishlist counter did not save American Cooking Simulator or Redhowl, the Steam CTA should be treated as a secondary conversion for non-backers, not as evidence the page will fund.

### Gaps
- The text printed *on* the banner images ("Wishlist on Steam", "Wishlist now", etc.) could not be read; all had empty alt text. Exact button wording is therefore unverified for every page.
- No data found on click-through or wishlist gain from these buttons.

## How is the core loop explained (numbered steps, role cards, "how a round works" graphics, GIF + caption pairs)?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is GIF + one or two sentences per beat, grouped under a "How It Works" / "Gameplay" header, with each side of an asymmetric game given its own short block. Numbered step lists are rare; role or faction blocks with one image each are common in social-deduction pages.

### Cited Findings
- **Deadly Trick** has the most explicit round walkthrough, under "How It Works": "5 to 8 players take part in a “Special Class.”" → "All players start as White, working together to complete the class activities." → "If a player picks up a weapon that spawns randomly on the map, that White becomes a Shadow." → "The Mastermind hides among the Whites." → "When a player is eliminated, a class trial begins." Each sentence sits between its own image or GIF. Phases then get emoji sub-headings: "🔍 Investigation phase", "⚖️ Trial phase" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/finalblow/deadly-trick-a-reasoning-based-social-deduction-game/description)
- **Carnival Hunt** explains the asymmetric loop side by side: the hook ("takes the classic "Hide & Seek" concept and infuses it with an exciting twist"), then a paragraph + GIF for the monster ("As a Carnival Monster, your initial goal is to prevent…"), then "On the other hand, the Bunnies' main objective is to escape", then level structure and an endgame twist ("When the first players escape, the Carnival Monster gains the ability to permanently eliminate Bunnies."). It also asks the reader to pick a side: "Will you embrace the role of the fearsome Carnival Monster, or will you join forces with your friends…" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/badraa/carnival-hunt/description)
- **Springfield Hotel** leads with the one mechanic that differs from the genre ("your role is shaped by what you find", then the Terror Rank noise system) before any feature list, and describes tasks as emoji-titled cards with one image each: "🍸 Spirited Service", "📖 Phantom Registry", "📡 Radio Relay", "🕰️ Haunted Heirlooms", ending "And many more..." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/description)
- **American Cooking Simulator** (failed) has a section literally titled "How a shift works": "During your shift, customers will approach and you will see their orders on the TV." Each gameplay section is heading + one image + one GIF + two or three lines — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description)
- **Redhowl** (failed) uses faction and role blocks: "Three factions clash" (Chaperons, Wolves, Pathless, two or three images each), "Thirteen uniques roles", "Gameplay mechanics" with one image per mechanic — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/benomstudio/redhowl/description)
- **The Boo Croo** explains through minigame length and feel rather than rules: "Each minigame lasts around three minutes and is designed to be replayable" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/description)
- **Mini Bash** gives each mini-game one sentence set as a heading plus two GIFs, e.g. "Grab your freeze-thrower or get ready to run because this game of Freeze Tag is cold." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/digitaldaylight/minibash/description)
- **Mischief House** (live, about 0% funded) explains the loop in prose under plain bold lines ("One of you is the Menace", "Sometimes everyone wants to be a problem") with one GIF and offers "a single-take, unedited recording of a complete Chaos match, so you can see exactly how the game plays right now." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ajamiz/mischief-house-do-chores-blame-your-friends/description)
- **Last One Lying** (failed) uses spec-sheet bullets: "6–12 players per match", "Short, intense rounds (approx. 5–8 minutes)", role lines "🟢 Innocents" / "🔴 Traitors" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/woidstudio/last-one-lying-social-deduction-party-game/description)
- **Drawnsters** ($157k, existing YouTube audience) has a "How to Play" section that is one graphic plus a guided-tour video with guests — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/pillowfightio/drawnsters/description)

### Inferences
- For an asymmetric 3-vs-1 game the closest working template is Carnival Hunt's: one hook sentence, then "as the hunter you…" and "as the hunted you…" blocks with a GIF each, then how a round ends. Deadly Trick's sentence-per-GIF round sequence is the clearest "how a round works" format and transfers directly (round start → fish tasks → shark hears voice → bite → escape or wipe).
- Springfield Hotel puts its differentiator (noise gives you away) third on the page, before features. Reef Run's equivalent ("the shark hears your voice chat") belongs in the same slot.
- No funded page in the set used a numbered 1-2-3 list for the loop; they sequence with images instead. A single "how a round works" graphic would be unusual but not contrary to anything seen.
- Round length and player count are stated as plain facts on several pages (Boo Croo "around three minutes", Last One Lying "5–8 minutes", Elevator "Quick Facts"); a small facts box is cheap and common.

### Gaps
- Whether any page uses a single infographic showing a full round could not be confirmed, since image contents were not read.
- No evidence was found linking a specific loop-explanation format to funding outcome; failed pages (American Cooking Simulator, Redhowl) used the same formats competently.

## How do multiplayer games answer "what if I have no friends to play with" and "will the servers be alive"?

### Takeaway
Most funded pages do not answer either question in the story at all. The few that do use three devices: a "solo or with friends" line backed by dynamic balancing or bots, a multi-copy tier, and (Fat Goblins only) a direct statement about server cost plus a promise of self-hosted servers.

### Cited Findings
- **Fat Goblins** is the only page that addresses server lifetime head-on: "Fat Goblins was already designed in a way with dynamic server scaling. When no one's playing, the cost drops to almost nothing", and "One Kickstarter goal is to fund the development of local-hosted servers. Even if the central servers go offline far in the future, you'll always be able to play with friends if this goal is reached!" Its Risks section names the problem: "The primary risk for Fat Goblins lies in its multiplayer focus. As with many indie multiplayer games, building and sustaining a large, a…" and the story says "These games live and die by their communities and replay value." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/kuju/fat-goblins-online-hat-based-pvp-inspired-by-fat-princess/description)
- Fat Goblins also answers "will anyone be there" with scheduled public events during the campaign: "A public, open-to-everyone playtest will run from September 24th to 28th at the start of the Kickstarter" and a Steam Next Fest slot "from October 13th to 20th" — same page.
- **American Cooking Simulator** (failed) has a section headed "Solo or with friends": "But if you don’t have a group ready, our dynamic balancing system allows you to enjoy the game solo as well." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description)
- **Rescue Ops: Wildfire:** "All missions can be played solo or in a squad of up to 4 players, with dynamic balancing based on your team size." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1556468747/rescue-ops-wildfire-a-co-op-firefighting-simulation-game/description)
- **BeeTheBest** (failed): heading "MULTIPLAYER OR SOLO" and "And what if it’s just you? Don’t fret, our bee-bots are just as numerous and engaging to play with! 🤖🐝" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/croissantcatgames/bee-the-best/description)
- **Elevator** states "can be played single or multiplayer" and prices for groups: "Elevator will be playable with lots of friends (up to 8 players), so we're pricing it around $7.99 USD." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/pixeltailgames/elevator-1/description)
- **Mischief House** (live) pitches a group purchase: "You can back the game for £6, or bring three frie…" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ajamiz/mischief-house-do-chores-blame-your-friends/description)
- **Last One Lying** (failed) lists "Online multiplayer with lobby-based matchmaking" and "Join via lobby code or server list" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/woidstudio/last-one-lying-social-deduction-party-game/description)
- **The Boo Croo** covers online reliability only in Risks: "We’re utilizing proven networking solutions, conducting frequent stress tests, and engaging outside expertise to ensure a seamless online experience worldwide." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/description)
- **Springfield Hotel** has no matchmaking, solo or server text in its live copy; a keyword pass for solo/matchmaking/bots/servers returned nothing — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/description)
- The project's earlier tier research found multi-copy tiers sell: Fat Goblins' $36 four-pack (limit 100) drew 77 backers, its second most-backed tier — recorded in `kickstarter/14_WHAT_SUCCESSFUL_CAMPAIGNS_DO.md` from the [Fat Goblins page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/kuju/fat-goblins-online-hat-based-pvp-inspired-by-fat-princess).
- A 2026 vendor playbook claims online multiplayer games are hard to crowdfund because backers struggle to value access to servers that might shut down — [Strayspark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/kickstarter-indie-game-campaign-playbook-2026) (marketing blog; opinion, seen as a search summary only).

### Inferences
- Because so few pages answer these questions, a short honest block is a cheap differentiator. Fat Goblins' formula is the model: say what hosting costs when idle, say what happens if the studio stops, and name the dates when strangers can play together.
- For a strictly 4-player game the working answers seen are: a four-copy tier (Fat Goblins, Mischief House), Discord as the place to find a group, and scheduled public playtests. Bots or solo modes are the other route (BeeTheBest, Rescue Ops, American Cooking Simulator) and only apply if Reef Run actually has them.
- If Reef Run uses Steam peer-to-peer/relay rather than paid dedicated servers, saying so plainly answers "will the servers be alive" better than any reassurance; no page in the set made that argument, so it would be new.

### Gaps
- FAQ tabs were not read for any campaign; some answers to these questions may live there rather than in the story.
- No page in the set described a "friend pass" or free-guest scheme; none was found.
- No data found on whether answering these questions changes conversion.

## What do team / "why Kickstarter" / "what's already done" sections look like on pages from tiny (1–3 person) teams?

### Takeaway
Tiny teams keep these sections short and personal: a first-person origin sentence, a concrete statement of what is already playable, and a short bullet list of what the money adds. The strongest trust signals are specifics (money and years already spent, a playable build, named dates), placed in the last third of the page.

### Cited Findings
- **Fat Goblins (solo developer):** origin in one line near the top, "Inspired by Fat Princess, a now-defunct game I loved as a kid". "Already done": "Indie games are risky, but Fat Goblins is already fully playable with large scale online PvP gameplay! We’ve even run public events…". "Why Kickstarter": "The main reasons for the Kickstarter are:" followed by four bullets — "Providing the runway and funding necessary to add more class unlocks and new game modes.", "Providing the funding for additional unique maps.", "Implementing the rune system for in-game progression.", "Better marketing to reach a wider audience." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/kuju/fat-goblins-online-hat-based-pvp-inspired-by-fat-princess/description)
- A campaign update quoted on Kicktraq says the Fat Goblins developer had been "developing this game solo for years" — [Kicktraq](https://www.kicktraq.com/projects/kuju/fat-goblins-online-hat-based-pvp-inspired-by-fat-princess/) (third-party tracker; seen as a search summary).
- **Springfield Hotel ("Team Story", founder-led):** "Hey there! My name is Frank. It’s my honor to lead the vision of Springfield Hotel." then a personal origin ("One night, I had a nightmare about being locked up in a sinister place") and the commitment line: "I quit my day job, put in all my savings ($135K USD spent in development so far), and built a team crazy enough to chase the idea with me." Followed by "Our Partners" and, in Risks, "we’re partnering with an Unreal Approved Service Partner for technical support" and "locked scope" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/description)
- **The Boo Croo ("Why Kickstarter?")** tells an event story rather than a budget story: "When we brought The Boo Croo to PAX West 2025 in Seattle, we had only a handful of minigames to showcase. That weekend changed everything." then three bullets ("Add more minigames packed with spooky surprises.", "Expand online play to make it seamless across regions.", "Create even more customization options…"). Team: "Synodic Arc is a small independent game development studio of 10 people based in Seattle, Washington. When we aren’t working at our day jobs, we’re hard at work on The Bo…". "Already done" sits in the roadmap: "core gameplay systems, several minigames, and our online prototype are up and running." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/description)
- **Elevator:** "Why We Need Help" is a bullet list starting "Helping fund Elevator means:" ("more floors (we have around 250 floor ideas already!)", "localization", "and more!"); "What We Have So Far" is a separate bullet list; the money framing is "we've gotten a lot of legwork on the game already and have a solid base, so we will use the funds to expand on what we have." Scope promises: "No Early Access." and "the development cycle shouldn’t go longer than a year." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/pixeltailgames/elevator-1/description)
- **Mischief House (solo, live, about 0%):** "The mess is already happening" — "Mischief House is playable and has already been through community playtests." with numbers: "There are already 1,000+ Steam wishlists, around 700 people in the Discord and 1.2k followers on TikTok." "Why Kickstarter?": "I’m ajamiz, the developer of Mischief House. A shoulder injury has slowed down the amount of development I can do myself." and "Funding will let me bring in an experienced Unity developer and an artist to help finish the game." Plus "I’ll post monthly updates showing what’s changed" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/ajamiz/mischief-house-do-chores-blame-your-friends/description)
- **Jousting Around (three people):** a dated week-by-week "Timeline" ("Weeks 3 - 4: Prepare Steam page.", "Week 8: Publish to Steam and launch final marketing campaign.") prefaced by "This one is tricky, and there is no guarantee. But there can be transparency." and an "Origins" story; the game "was originally created in 6 days" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/harper-rhett/jousting-around/description)
- **Cleaning Raccrew:** "The game is mostly done, and since the release date is already quite close" and a single stretch goal — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/teambrije/cleaning-raccrew/description)
- **American Cooking Simulator (failed, small student team):** "We’ve financed the project through savings, side jobs, and a very student-friendly lifestyle." / "But to finish the game at the quality level it deserves, we need to focus on it full-time." Risks: "That could mean a later release date, but not a cancelled game." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description)
- **Redhowl (failed, two founders):** "It all began 10 years ago, when Jordan and I met as part of an amateur esports team." and "The game is now close to release, planned for the end of the year." — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/benomstudio/redhowl/description)
- **Red Pine Lake (failed)** frames the money as hiring to start the work: "We need funding to get a dedicated 3D artist onboard", "We also need another dedicated programmer", and admits "We are in early stages at the moment and the footage we have shown is pre alpha" — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/sakurastudios/red-pine-lake-asym-survival-horror/description)

### Inferences
- The working shape for a two-person team: (a) two or three first-person sentences on who you are and why this game, with a photo; (b) a "what's already playable" bullet list with numbers; (c) "the Kickstarter pays for:" three or four bullets of additions; (d) a by-function budget graphic; (e) a dated roadmap. Fat Goblins and Elevator both phrase the money as extending a playable game, not rescuing an unfinished one.
- Funds framed as "finish/expand what you can already play" appear on funded pages; funds framed as "hire the people who will build it" (Red Pine Lake) or "let us go full-time" (American Cooking Simulator) appear on failed ones. Mischief House's "bring in a developer and an artist" framing is closer to the second group and is so far at about 0%; this is suggestive only, with small numbers.
- Specific sunk cost works as a trust signal (Springfield's "$135K USD spent"). Reef Run's equivalent is hours/weeks built and playtest counts.
- Team sections sit in the last third of every funded page read; none opens with the team.

### Gaps
- Team size is unstated on several pages (BLACKOUT, Deadly Trick, Bold Club beyond "small team of friends"), so the "tiny team" sample is thin: Fat Goblins, Jousting Around, Mischief House, Redhowl.
- No 2025–2026 creator postmortem from a one-to-three-person multiplayer video-game campaign was found. Searches returned older or tabletop postmortems and vendor blogs.

## What do pages that FAILED in this niche have in common on the page itself (not just audience)?

### Takeaway
There is no single on-page defect: several failed pages (American Cooking Simulator, Redhowl, Blue Ridge Hunting, BeeTheBest) are well built, with demos, GIFs, Steam buttons and clear loop explanations, and still reached only 16–41% of goal. Where failed pages do differ on the page, it is in scope sprawl, early-stage footage, money framed as hiring, missing Steam/Discord links, and text-first layouts.

### Cited Findings
- **Well-made pages that failed.** American Cooking Simulator: 43 images, at least 9 GIFs, demo button first, "How a shift works", public counters showing 3,441 wishlists and 3,852 demo players; raised €6,382 of €20,000. Redhowl: 49 images, "PLAY THE DEMO" first, proximity voice chat, "close to release"; raised €645 of €4,000. Blue Ridge Hunting relaunch: 34 images, free Steam demo with online multiplayer, an "EVENT CALENDAR" of reveals, voiced cast, lower goal than the first attempt; raised $2,879 of $10,000. BeeTheBest: 59 images, at least 11 GIFs, demo; raised €4,432 of €10,890 — [American Cooking Simulator](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/freedomburgergames/american-cooking-simulator-multiplayer/description), [Redhowl](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/benomstudio/redhowl/description), [Blue Ridge Hunting](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/jadecompany/blue-ridge-hunting-co-op-appalachia-cryptid-hunting/description), [BeeTheBest](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/croissantcatgames/bee-the-best/description)
- **Scope sprawl.** Red Pine Lake pitches three products at once under "Gameplay": a single-player "Story Campaign", a co-op "Horde Mode" and the asymmetric "Infected Mode" ("Our flagship asymmetrical mode"), plus "a whole franchise, starting here". Blue Ridge Hunting splits the page between "FREEPLAY MODE" and a five-chapter "STORY MODE". BeeTheBest pitches Arena, Quiz and Boss modes and an educational goal ("Who said videogames and learning don’t go together?") — [Red Pine Lake](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/sakurastudios/red-pine-lake-asym-survival-horror/description), [Blue Ridge Hunting](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/jadecompany/blue-ridge-hunting-co-op-appalachia-cryptid-hunting/description), [BeeTheBest](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/croissantcatgames/bee-the-best/description)
- **Early-stage admission and hiring framing.** Red Pine Lake: "We are in early stages at the moment and the footage we have shown is pre alpha", "all screenshots and gameplay footage are from early builds", and funds to hire a 3D artist and a programmer; no Steam or Discord link in the story — [page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/sakurastudios/red-pine-lake-asym-survival-horror/description)
- **No Steam link / thin media.** Last One Lying: 11 images, 4 GIFs, no Steam link, a Discord invite, about 1,200 words of mostly text and bullet specs; it opens with three paragraphs of abstract premise ("A group of people is placed into an isolated environment where there is no outside authority…") before any image. LiniMall: 32 images, 0 GIFs, no Steam or Discord link, "Stretch Goals" as the second section of a $50k-goal page that raised $767 — [Last One Lying](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/woidstudio/last-one-lying-social-deduction-party-game/description), [LiniMall](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/creativenamestudios/linimall/description)
- **Stretch goals placed before the game is explained** appear on failed pages (LiniMall second section; Redhowl second section; Blue Ridge Hunting before modes) but also on funded ones (The Bold Club, Combo Devils) — pages as linked above.
- **Language switchers at the very top.** BeeTheBest opens with three blocks pointing to Spanish and Catalan versions before any pitch; Redhowl opens with "📌​ Page traduite en Français ici" — [BeeTheBest](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/croissantcatgames/bee-the-best/description), [Redhowl](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/benomstudio/redhowl/description)
- **Derivative positioning.** Last One Lying defines itself by another game ("we love Trouble in Terrorist Town — and we love 2D games"); Redhowl is a werewolf-format game ("Inspired by the tale of Little R…") in a crowded format — pages as linked above.
- Context from the project's own dataset: across 934 failed video-game campaigns in the 12 months to 20 Oct 2026, the median reached 1.9% of goal and 75% reached under 10% — `kickstarter/14_WHAT_SUCCESSFUL_CAMPAIGNS_DO.md`, built from [Kickstarter Discover](https://www.kickstarter.com/discover/advanced?category_id=35&state=failed&sort=end_date).
- GameDiscoverCo's review of 2025 video-game crowdfunding calls community building "the cornerstone of crowdfunding" and says paid ads "can only complement" it; it cites Autonomica launching with over 10,000 Kickstarter followers — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding)

### Inferences
- The clearest reading is negative: a good page with a demo is necessary but not sufficient. American Cooking Simulator had about 3,400 wishlists and a strong page and reached 32% of a €20k goal; Mischief House has about 1,000 wishlists and is at about 0% of €14.4k. For Reef Run this means wishlist counts in the low thousands do not by themselves predict a €14k raise.
- Goal size relative to audience looks decisive in the pairs seen: Fat Goblins ($8k, funded in under 24 hours) and Boo Croo ($10k) set goals their audience covered early; American Cooking Simulator (€20k) and Red Pine Lake (£15k) drew similar or larger backer money than some funded pages (£9.2k from 58 backers) and still failed.
- On-page habits to avoid, drawn from the failed set: pitching several modes or products with equal weight; saying the footage is pre-alpha; asking for money to hire the people who will build the game; opening with abstract premise text or language notices instead of a GIF and a one-line pitch; omitting Steam/Discord buttons; no GIFs.
- Social-deduction pages that failed (Last One Lying, Redhowl) did not show a mechanic that differs from the format they copy. Springfield Hotel, in the same genre, led with its differentiator ("The Hotel is Always Listening"). Reef Run's voice-eavesdropping shark is that kind of differentiator and should sit in the first three blocks.

### Gaps
- Pre-launch follower counts, email list sizes and day-one figures for the failed campaigns are unknown, so audience and page effects cannot be separated.
- Videos of failed campaigns were not watched; trailer quality may be a shared weakness that this pass could not see.
- Only seven failed pages were read, chosen from those that raised at least $500; the far more numerous near-zero failures were not sampled and probably show more basic page defects.
- No creator postmortems for these specific failed campaigns were found.
