# 01 — Market Research

Research date: **2026-10-17**. Source IDs (K#, S#, M#) refer to `research/SOURCES.md`. Steam numbers come from the
official store API. Player counts come from SteamCharts. Kickstarter numbers come from Kickstarter's own Discover data.

> **How to read the numbers.** *Reviews* are counted exactly. *Estimated units* = reviews × 20–60 (M8): a rough
> industry rule with ±30–50% error, and it's meaningless for free-to-play or giveaway titles. *Concurrent players* are
> not sales. *Crowdfunding success* is not commercial success. Anything not verified is marked **unknown**.

## 1. Summary of findings

1. **The demand is real, but it sits on Steam and in streams, not on Kickstarter.** Friend-group co-op and party games
   with proximity voice are among Steam's biggest indie hits of 2023–2025 (*Lethal Company* 511k reviews, *R.E.P.O.*
   429k, *PEAK* 380k, *Content Warning* 164k). **None of them was crowdfunded.** They grew through Steam, cheap prices
   ($4.95–$9.99) and streamer clips. *Among Us* averaged 30–50 concurrent players for two years before streamers
   picked it up (M3).
2. **Multiplayer party and social-deduction video games do poorly on Kickstarter without an existing audience.** Of
   the 29 such campaigns pulled (§3.2), most that succeeded raised under $25k. The big wins had a licence (*Friday the
   13th*, $824k) or a pre-built community. *Hide Or Die* ($155k) and *Carnival Hunt* ($122k) are the realistic upper
   reference points for an original asymmetric multiplayer game.
3. **Competitive asymmetric PvP that depends on matchmaking dies fast.** *Deceive Inc.*, *Midnight Ghost Hunt* and
   *Depth* had thousands of concurrent players at their peak and now have 9–16 (§2). Friend-lobby games last longer.
   **REEF RUN should be sold as a game you play *with your friends*, not as a competitive matchmaking game.**
4. **Shark and ocean themes have proven appeal.** *Depth* (sharks vs divers, about 33k reviews), *Maneater* (27k) and
   *Feed and Grow: Fish* (22k) show it. On Kickstarter, ocean games with cozy or adventure positioning do well (*Summer
   in Mara* $268k, *Tomo Endless Blue* $253k in 2026, *Tideborne Haven* $194k in 2026). Underwater action and survival
   campaigns have mostly failed (*The Big Blue*, *Submerge*, *EXONAUTIS*, *The Great Barrier*).
5. **Platform constraints that shape the plan:**
   - The creator is resident in the Netherlands, which is eligible (K3); the campaign raises in EUR.
   - Steam keys can't be promised until the game has confirmed Steam distribution (S3).
   - The campaign must run at most 30 days, with no late pledges before the Steam release (S3).
6. **Recommendation:** position REEF RUN as **"the proximity-chat shark game for you and three friends"**. Treat
   Kickstarter as an **optional, small, late** funding step that happens only if demand gates are met after a Steam
   page and a public playtest. The case for launching on Kickstarter today is weak (see §6, and 12_LAUNCH_READINESS_AUDIT).

**Takeaways (medium-high confidence):**
- Original multiplayer party and social-deduction games without a pre-built audience mostly fail, or fund under
  $25k. Even the successes are small.
- The upper band for an original asymmetric MP game is about **$80–155k** (Springfield Hotel, Carnival Hunt, Hide Or
  Die). The comparable average pledge there was **$63–110**, driven by many mid and high personalisation tiers.
- The successful ocean campaigns were **cozy or adventure, single-player-leaning**. That audience overlaps only
  partly with a predator party game.
- 2025 context: 443 successful video-game Kickstarters raised about $26M combined (M1). That's under $60k per success
  on average, and the median is lower.

### 3.3 Reward patterns seen (K22)

- **The most-backed tiers are the cheapest "get the game" tiers:** Friday the 13th $25/$30 (4,565 backers), Hide Or Die
  $20 (866), Ahoy £60 with beta (1,960), Carnival Hunt $35 BETA (271). Early-bird limited versions sell out first.
- **Friend multi-packs exist in every multiplayer campaign:** F13 2-pack and 4-pack, Carnival Hunt "BETA Quartet",
  Ahoy 2/3/4/5-key officer tiers. For a 4-player game this is the natural centre tier.
- **Personalisation tiers** (name on a tombstone, custom character) raise the average but each one is a content task.
  Hide Or Die offered seven of them.
- **Physical rewards** appear only in licensed or large campaigns (F13 physical editions).

## 7. Is Kickstarter appropriate right now?

**No, not yet.** The evidence:
1. ~~Eligibility~~ resolved: the creator lives in the Netherlands (2026-10-17). Identity and bank verification are still to do.
2. **Keys:** Steam keys can't be promised until a Steam app exists (S3). Today the game runs on App 480.
3. **Audience:** there is none (00 §7). Comparable original-MP campaigns without an audience failed (§3.2).
4. **Stability:** the critical client-side bug undermines a public demo or playtest.
5. **Product-market fit:** the genre's winners sold on Steam, not Kickstarter. A Steam page, a playtest and Next Fest
   give stronger demand evidence at lower cost.

**When it would become appropriate** (gates are detailed in 02):
- eligibility is solved;
- the Steam page is live;
- the bug is fixed and a public playtest has run;
- there are **≥7,500 wishlists** and **≥1,500 Kickstarter pre-launch followers** (or an equivalent mix with email subscribers);
- a playtest survey shows **≥40% "would buy" among people who played 3+ rounds**.

Those thresholds are reasoned in 02 §7, and they are assumptions to revisit.

**What Kickstarter could still add even then:** a modest cash injection (see 03), a committed beta community (the
playtesters a 4-player game needs), and a public demand signal for press. It is *not* the main revenue event. The
Steam launch is.
