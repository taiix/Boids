# Rewards, stretch goals, budget, risks, FAQ and AI disclosure on 2025–2026 Kickstarter video game pages

Research date: 2026-11-16. Method notes for the report writer:

- Two live Kickstarter pages were read directly in a browser (story, rewards tab, FAQ tab): **Springfield Hotel** (the named competitor) and **The Boo Croo** (a 4-player online party game with a $10k goal, the closest size match to Reef Run found). Text quoted from them is verbatim. Graphics on those pages were read from screenshots where stated; anything in an image I did not open is listed under Gaps.
- Search access was rate-limited for part of the session, so coverage is narrower than planned. No Reddit threads, BackerKit/Launchboom posts or Kicktraq daily curves were retrieved. Those are marked as gaps, not filled from memory.
- Items marked **UNVERIFIED** come from search-result summaries of secondary sites that I could not open or that give no method.

## Rewards: how digital-only tiers are presented, early-bird limits, group packs, name-in-game tiers, delivery wording

### Takeaway
Both comparable 2026 campaigns present tiers as a custom graphic of stacked cards in the story (price badge, tier name, one-line content, "Limited to N") and repeat the detail in Kickstarter's reward cards. For a multiplayer game the multi-copy packs carried the campaign at Springfield Hotel (2-, 4- and 8-packs = 42% of backers, each pack cheaper per seat and with the per-seat price printed), while a 4-pack priced above the single-copy rate at The Boo Croo drew 6% of backers.

### Cited Findings

**Springfield Hotel (Mar 17 – May 1 2026, 45 days, $84,908 from 771 backers on a $10,000 goal, "Project We Love", Los Angeles)** — all from [Springfield Hotel campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game) and its [rewards tab](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/rewards):

- Only five tiers, all digital, top price $99. No $1/$5 thank-you tier, no tier above $99, no design-a-character tier.
- "Just the game" $15: "50% OFF, Saving $15 from MSRP!" / "+ Early Access / + All stretch goals." — 56 backers.
- "Kickstarter Exclusive" $19: "Get ALL the Kickstarter Exclusive items for just $4 more!" Includes alpha and Early Access, three weapon/flashlight skins, "Unique Discord Role", "In-Game Name Credit", "All stretch goals". Limited to 385, 380 claimed ("5 left of 385"). This is the most-backed tier (49% of backers).
- "Deluxe Duo Pack (2x)" $29, limit 320, 187 backers: "Find a friend to split, you can save another $4.5 per person, plus a friend to play with! ($14.5 ea)".
- "Deluxe Quad Pack (4x)" $55, limit 320, 107 backers, marked "Featured": "Find friends to split, you can save another $5.25 per person, plus some friends to play with! ($13.75 ea)".
- "Deluxe Mega Pack (8x)" $99, unlimited, 27 backers: "($12.37 ea)".
- Every tier anchors on a discount from the planned store price ("64% OFF, Saving $97 from MSRP").
- Every pack tier adds one cosmetic the single tier lacks ("The only thing this tier doesn't have is the Party Hat.").
- Delivery wording on every tier: "Estimated delivery Dec 2026" (about 7 months after the campaign ended). Items listed as "Game (Steam Key)" and "Game (Alpha Access)".
- The story page has a "Reward Tiers" section and a "Kickstarter Exclusives" section; both are graphics (no text in the page body).
- Tier backers visible on the rewards tab sum to 757 of 771 backers. Tier price × backers sums to about $22,000, far below the $84,908 total. The page shows "optional add-ons" on every tier and a "Go to pledge manager" button. I could not determine where the remaining ~$63,000 came from.
- An earlier snapshot showed $57,823 from 588 backers — [PledgeBox listing](https://www.pledgebox.com/projects/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game) (seen in a search summary; page itself would not load).

**The Boo Croo – A Chaotic 4-Player Party Game (Feb 23 – Mar 25 2026, 30 days, $15,640 from 416 backers on a $10,000 goal, 10-person part-time team, Seattle)** — all from [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game) and its [rewards tab](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/rewards):

- Tier ladder and backers: $5 "Ghostly Gratitude" (no game; credits name, backer voting, Discord role) 7 backers; $12 "Early Boo Buddy" limit 250, 182 claimed (68 left at the end); $15 "Boo Buddy" 75; $22 "Early Croo Member" limit 250, 50 claimed; $25 "Croo Member" 18; $50 "Spectral Supporter" (beta access + signed print) 15; $80 "Assemble Your Boo Croo" (4 copies) 25; $89 early plushie bundle limit 50, 26 claimed; $99 plushie bundle 8; $400 "Fashionable Field" (design a cosmetic with the team, with credit) limit 15, 2 claimed; $800 "Legend of the Boo Croo" (backer count not captured).
- The $12 early bird did not sell out in 30 days (182 of 250), yet it was still the most-backed tier (44% of backers). $12 + $15 single-copy tiers together = 62% of backers.
- The 4-pack at $80 is $20 per copy, more than the $15 single copy; it drew 25 backers (6%).
- Story wording for early birds: "To thank our earliest backers, a limited number of Early Bird slots will be available at launch! Once these are gone, they're gone!"
- Tier graphic style (read from screenshot): one rounded card per tier, price in a badge at top-left, tier name, one line such as "A discounted bundle of the 'Boo Buddy' reward tier.", and "Limited to 250" under the card. Early-bird cards are grouped first, then a "Rewards" header with the note that each tier includes rewards from previous tiers.
- Name-in-credits is in every tier from $5, split into named credit sections by tier ("Friendly Spirits", "Haunting the Halls", "The Boo Croo Heroes").
- Add-on: "Additional Digital Copy +€14" on every tier (shown in the viewer's currency).
- Delivery wording: "Estimated delivery Sep 2026" on all tiers (6 months after the campaign ended).
- Average pledge: $15,640 / 416 = about $37.60, lifted by plushie tiers (my calculation from the page figures).

**Market context**

- 2025: 443 successful video game campaigns raised about $26m on Kickstarter, an average of $58,000 per campaign; 11 campaigns passed $500k — [GameDiscoverCo, citing Thomas Bidaux of ICO](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- "Most successful campaigns raise $10,000 to $50,000" — [Cinevva guide, 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games) (secondary guide; no dataset given).

### Inferences
- **Reef Run €5 tier:** fine to keep, but expect almost nothing from it (7 of 416 at Boo Croo; Springfield had none and lost nothing).
- **Reef Run €12 early bird, limit 300 / €15 regular:** almost identical to Boo Croo ($12 limit 250 / $15). Boo Croo's did not sell out with 416 total backers. A limit of 300 at a €14k goal means the early bird may never close, which removes the scarcity it is meant to create and puts most backers at €12 instead of €15. At €12 average, €14k needs about 1,170 backers; Boo Croo got 416 and Springfield 771. A lower limit (roughly 100–150) or a time-boxed early bird fits the evidence better. This is a judgement from two cases, not a measured effect.
- **Reef Run €49 four-pack:** €12.25 per seat, i.e. no cheaper than the early bird and only 18% under the regular price. Springfield's packs dropped the per-seat price at every step and printed it; they took 42% of backers. Boo Croo's pack cost more per seat than a single copy and took 6%. Reef Run needs four players, so the pack is the natural hero tier: print the per-seat price, give the pack one small exclusive cosmetic, and consider a 2-pack (Springfield's 2-pack outsold its 4-pack 187 to 107).
- **Reef Run €29 tier:** Boo Croo's $22/$25 "game + extras" tiers drew 68 backers together (16%); Springfield's best seller was a +$4 upgrade over the base game. A small step-up sells; €29 is nearly double the base and sits where a 2-pack would, so it risks being skipped unless its contents are clearly worth €14 more.
- **Reef Run €99 and €199 (limit 25):** Boo Croo's $400 design-a-cosmetic tier sold 2 of 15. Springfield offered nothing above $99 and still passed $84k. Plan revenue as if 2–5 of the 25 sell; a limit of 10 would look fuller.
- **Delivery date wording:** both pages use Kickstarter's standard "Estimated delivery <Month Year>" set 6–7 months after campaign end and name the deliverable "Steam Key". Springfield separates "Alpha Access" and "Early Access" as items.

### Gaps
- Springfield's "Reward Tiers" and "Kickstarter Exclusives" graphics were not opened, so whether it uses a comparison chart or cards is unconfirmed.
- No statistical source on tier comparison charts vs cards vs tables was found.
- No 2025–2026 dataset on most-backed price points for digital PC games was found; the two cases above are the only evidence.
- Why Springfield's total is about four times its visible tier revenue is unexplained.

## Stretch goals: ladders for €5k–€25k goals, what is unlocked, reveal graphics, community goals, delivery risk

### Takeaway
The Boo Croo's ladder at 125 / 150 / 200 / 250 / 500 / 750% of a $10k goal is nearly the same shape as Reef Run's 125 / 150 / 200 / 250 / 400%; the campaign ended at 156% and unlocked only the first two steps. Kickstarter itself has warned that stretch goals leave projects "overwhelmed, over-budget, and behind schedule"; no controlled study isolating stretch goals as a cause of late delivery was found.

### Cited Findings
- Boo Croo ladder, read from the page's stretch-goal graphic (a single tall winding-path infographic, every goal visible from the start, no locked or hidden goals): $10K "Fully Funded!"; $12.5K "Haunted Tunes" (professionally mastered soundtrack); $15K "More Mayhem" (3 new minigames voted for by backers); $20K "Ghosts Worldwide" (localization into 7+ languages, flags shown: ES, FR, DE, JP, CH, KR); $25K "Spirited Style Pack" (10+ costumes, hats and effects plus one more Kickstarter-exclusive cosmetic); $50K "Console Haunting" (Switch, PS5, Xbox Series X ports); $75K "Remix Mode" — [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo intro wording: "We're keeping things transparent, realistic, and fun. Every dollar you pledge goes toward making The Boo Croo bigger, spookier, and even more chaotic. Our starting goal is $10,000 to complete the core game: finalize 12+ minigames, polish visuals, and deliver smooth online & local play for PC (Steam)." — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo final: $15,640, so $12.5K and $15K unlocked; localization, cosmetics, console and remix mode did not — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo "Community Goals" (separate graphic, each unlocks one cosmetic): "500 Discord Members — Unlocks 'Gamer Shades' Face Cosmetic Piece"; "20 Social Media Image Mentions — Unlocks 'Chef' Torso Cosmetic Piece"; "1000 Facebook Followers" (chef hat). Intro: "We're offering additional content to the base game if we meet certain community goals! Share with your friends and help us grow our community!" — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo let people pledge for a console copy before the console stretch goal was reached, and had to add the FAQ "What if I select a console copy and we don't reach the stretch goal for console porting?" — [Boo Croo FAQ](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/faqs).
- Springfield Hotel has a "Stretch Goals" section (graphic) and writes "+ All stretch goals." into every reward tier, so backers see that unlocked content is included at every price — [Springfield Hotel rewards tab](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/rewards).
- Kickstarter co-founder Yancey Strickler (2013, older): "We've seen stretch goals leave some projects overwhelmed, over-budget, and behind schedule." and "More money means more backers and rewards to fulfill — and less margin for error," and "expanding a project's scope can change the creative vision and put the whole project at risk." — [Game Informer, 13 Aug 2013](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2013/08/13/kickstarter-warns-that-stretch-goals-muddy-the-waters.aspx).
- Older delay data (pre-2025): a Cliqist analysis of 371 Kickstarter games released 2009–2015 found 285 shipped later than the estimate on their reward tiers — [Cliqist, 2017](https://cliqist.com/2017/06/02/kickstarters-delayed-reaction-many-games-late/) (figure from a search summary, article not opened: **UNVERIFIED**).
- Advice in a 2026 guide: "Fund fast, then stretch" — ask for what is needed to finish, then add stretch goals — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games).
- Risk-section wording that ties scope to stretch goals: "We've locked the scope to avoid feature creep. 12+ minigames at launch, with extras handled via stretch goals or free updates." — [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).

### Inferences
- Reef Run's percent steps match what a same-size 2026 campaign used. On Boo Croo's result, only the 125% and 150% steps are likely to be reached; put the two things backers most want there and make them cheap to build (Boo Croo: soundtrack mastering, backer-voted content).
- Reef Run's 400% step (€56k) is in the range Boo Croo did not come near (its $50K step was 3.2x its final total). Showing it is harmless if it is an obviously big item, but it should not carry anything backers assume is in the base game.
- Do not sell a reward that depends on an unreached stretch goal (the Boo Croo console-copy FAQ shows the confusion it causes).
- Showing every goal up front worked for both campaigns. I found no 2025–2026 small campaign whose ladder I could confirm used locked/"???" reveals, so there is no evidence either way on hidden goals.
- Community goals that unlock a single cosmetic each are a low-cost pattern and do not add delivery risk.
- "Stretch goals hurt delivery" is supported by platform statement and case studies, not by a controlled study. State each goal's added time on the page, or state that goals ship as post-launch free updates.

### Gaps
- Springfield Hotel's stretch-goal graphic was not opened; its steps and what they unlocked are unknown.
- No 2025–2026 study linking stretch goal count to delivery delay was found.
- No evidence was collected on hidden/locked reveal graphics or on which unlock types move pledges.

## Budget and "Why Kickstarter?": wording and graphics from small teams; stating unpaid founder time

### Takeaway
The small-team pattern is a six-slice pie chart with round percentages plus one sentence, and a "Why Kickstarter?" section that says what already exists and lists what the money adds. Springfield Hotel states the founder's own money spent ("$135K USD spent in development so far") in the team story; I found no 2025–2026 example that states unpaid founder hours in the budget itself.

### Cited Findings
- Boo Croo "Funding Breakdown" (pie chart with icon legend, read from screenshot): Development & Programming 30%, Art & Animation 30%, Audio & Music 10%, Marketing & Community 10%, Rewards & Shipping 10%, Contingency & Fees 10%. Caption: "Every bit of funding goes directly into development and community engagement. No corporate ghosts here!" — [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game). (The first legend row was partly cut off in the screenshot; 30% is read from the slice and the remaining total.)
- Boo Croo "Why Kickstarter?" wording: "When we brought The Boo Croo to PAX West 2025 in Seattle, we had only a handful of minigames to showcase. That weekend changed everything. Players kept returning to try them all..." followed by a five-item list (more minigames, online play across regions, more customization, polish, console versions) and "Backing this project is not only about helping us finish the game, it is about joining the Croo." — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo on unpaid time: "Synodic Arc is a small independent game development studio of 10 people based in Seattle, Washington. When we aren't working at our day jobs, we're hard at work on The Boo Croo." — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Boo Croo roadmap: "Development Roadmap & Timeline" graphic of phase cards starting with "Prototype & Core Systems", introduced by "We've already laid the foundation for The Boo Croo: core gameplay systems, several minigames, and our online prototype are up and running. With your help, we'll take it all the way to launch and beyond!" — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Springfield Hotel on founder investment: "I quit my day job, put in all my savings ($135K USD spent in development so far), and built a team crazy enough to chase the idea with me." It also has a "How We Plan To Use Your Funding" section (graphic) — [Springfield Hotel campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game).
- Worked example of what a small raise leaves: Space Chef raised $50,000 from 1,119 backers; about $20,000 went to marketing, ads and creators and about $4,000 to fees and taxes, leaving $26,000 for development. Kickstarter takes about 5% and payment processing about 3–5% — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games) (secondary; original Space Chef post-mortem not opened).
- "Most successful campaigns cover only 25–50% of development costs" — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games) (no dataset given).
- Backer caution about distant releases: "the further away that moment is from the launch of the game, the more it might evolve from the promises of the campaign" (Thomas Bidaux) — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).

### Inferences
- A goal of $10k–€14k cannot be presented as "the budget of the game". Both pages frame it as finishing money on top of work already done (Boo Croo: "to complete the core game"; Springfield: $135K already spent). Reef Run should say what exists today and what €14,000 specifically pays for.
- Stating founder contribution as a concrete fact (money or months already put in, "no salaries are paid from this campaign") follows the Springfield pattern and answers the obvious question of how two people live on €14k. I have no backer-reaction evidence for an "unpaid hours" line specifically.
- A fees line is expected: Boo Croo shows "Contingency & Fees 10%". For a Dutch creator, VAT and Kickstarter's ~8–10% fees should be visible in the chart rather than hidden in "development".

### Gaps
- Springfield's funding graphic was not opened.
- No backer comments reacting to budget sections were collected (comments tabs not read).
- No example found of a tiny team stating unpaid founder time in hours or as an explicit budget line.
- Kickstarter's own guidance on budget transparency could not be opened (the Creator Handbook returned HTTP 403).

## Risks and challenges: honest examples from online multiplayer games; Kickstarter's guidance

### Takeaway
Kickstarter's prompt is "What are the risks and challenges this project faces, and what qualifies you to overcome them?". The two comparable multiplayer campaigns answer in under 100 words each, name netcode, scope and schedule, and pair every risk with a mitigation; neither mentions server costs, player-count dependence or what happens if the team stops, which leaves room for Reef Run to be more candid than its competitors.

### Cited Findings
- The mandatory section's question: "What are the risks and challenges this project faces, and what qualifies you to overcome them?" — [NPR, 21 Sep 2012 (older)](https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2012/09/21/161587229/kickstarter-says-it-is-not-a-store-as-it-revises-policy-on-projects-risks) (wording taken from a search summary of this coverage).
- Boo Croo, full text: "Even ghosts hit a few bumps in the night, but we've planned ahead. **Online Multiplayer** We're utilizing proven networking solutions, conducting frequent stress tests, and engaging outside expertise to ensure a seamless online experience worldwide. **Scope & Schedule** We've locked the scope to avoid feature creep. 12+ minigames at launch, with extras handled via stretch goals or free updates. **Physical Rewards** We have partnered with reliable fulfillment teams to securely and on time ship physical products, such as our plushies. **Transparency** Expect monthly updates with progress clips, dev insights, and honest communication if anything changes." — [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).
- Springfield Hotel, full text: "Making a multiplayer game like Springfield Hotel is complex, especially when balancing art, design, and online systems. To ensure quality, we're partnering with an Unreal Approved Service Partner for technical support, while our core team handles all creative direction and production. Our biggest risks are scheduling delays and technical hurdles. However, we've built a clear roadmap, locked scope, and regular milestone reviews to keep us on track. If challenges arise, we'll communicate openly with backers through transparent progress updates. Your support helps us maintain the quality, polish, and consistency this project deserves." — [Springfield Hotel campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game).
- Springfield also fills Kickstarter's "Environmental commitments" block: "we do not produce physical copies of the game. All deliveries of game copies will be sent via digital platforms to reduce carbon footprint." — [same page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game).
- Kickstarter states that backers cannot expect refunds from the platform when a project fails; a funded game studio can still close without delivering: Alzara raised about €300,000 from over 5,000 backers and the studio shut down in April 2025 without a game or refunds — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games); also reported by [Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/studio-raised-300000-euros-for-a-role-playing-game-on-steam-shuts-down-a-year-later-and-leaves-fans-with-nothing,1398765) (second article seen as a search result only).
- Older research: about 12% of video game Kickstarter projects fail to deliver; controlling for size, games are no more likely to fail than other projects of the same size — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/12-of-video-game-kickstarter-campaigns-fail-to-deliver) (older; from search summary).
- An academic working paper on the introduction of the risks section reported that projects received fewer pledges after it was added — [Kelley School working paper](https://kelley.iu.edu/doc/bloomington/news-events/events/bloomington/midwest-account-research-conference/mm-disclosure-tar-3-30.pdf) (partial snippet only: **UNVERIFIED**, and about 2012, not about how a section is written).

### Inferences
- The observed structure is: one framing sentence, then 3–4 bolded risk names, each with one mitigation sentence, ending with a communication promise (monthly updates).
- Risks specific to Reef Run that neither competitor addresses and that a careful backer will think of: (1) it needs four players, so a small player base means empty lobbies; (2) two people, so one illness stops work; (3) online play and proximity voice depend on third-party services (Steam networking/voice); (4) the delivery date. Naming these with the concrete mitigation (Steam-hosted peer-to-peer or relay so there is no server bill to stop paying, the 4-pack and friend invites, bots or a practice mode if they exist, a buffer in the delivery month) would be more specific than either quoted example. Whether each mitigation is true for Reef Run must be checked by the team; I am not asserting them.
- Springfield's "locked scope" claim sits beside a stretch-goal ladder; Boo Croo resolves that tension explicitly ("extras handled via stretch goals or free updates"). Reef Run should use the Boo Croo form.

### Gaps
- Kickstarter's current Creator Handbook text on the risks section could not be opened (HTTP 403), so "what Kickstarter says a good risks section contains" rests on the 2012 question wording only.
- No example was found of a 2025–2026 online multiplayer campaign that discusses server costs or player-count dependence in its risks section.
- No backer comments reacting to risk sections were collected.

## FAQ: questions multiplayer PC game pages answer most

### Takeaway
The Boo Croo's 15-question FAQ is a usable checklist for a multiplayer Steam game; Springfield Hotel passed $84k with only two FAQ entries (Early Access vs full release, platform). Neither page has an FAQ entry on refunds, crossplay, Steam Deck, bots/solo play or AI.

### Cited Findings
- Boo Croo FAQ questions, verbatim and in page order: "When will the game be released?"; "What platforms will the game be released on?"; "What if I select a console copy and we don't reach the stretch goal for console porting?"; "Is there a demo of the game I can try?"; "How will funding be used?"; "Is there exclusive content for Kickstarter backers?"; "What happens if the game is delayed?"; "What languages will the game be localized for?"; "Is there a multiplayer or online mode?"; "When will I receive my Steam Key?"; "How can I stay updated on development?"; "Can I provide feedback during development?"; "When will I receive my Discord Role/Channel Access?"; "Will my money be collected if the Kickstarter campaign is not successful?"; "When will my physical rewards be shipped?" — [Boo Croo FAQ](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/faqs).
- Springfield Hotel FAQ questions: "Is Early Access the same as a full release?"; "What platform will the game be on?" — [Springfield Hotel FAQ](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/faqs).
- House House put its AI statement in the game's FAQ rather than on the store page — [Dexerto, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/big-walk-review-bombed-after-house-house-admits-ai-touched-well-under-0-1-of-code-3427480/).

### Inferences
- Recurring themes across the two pages: release date, platform, key delivery timing, what backers get that others do not, delays, languages, how to follow and give feedback, and the all-or-nothing charge rule.
- Questions on the brief's list that neither page answers (player count per match, solo/bots, crossplay, Steam Deck, refunds) are open ground. For a game that requires four players and uses proximity voice, "Can I play with fewer than 4?", "Do I need a microphone?" and "Can my friends join if only I backed?" are likely first questions; this is my inference, not an observed frequency.
- An FAQ entry "Did you use AI?" is where a detailed AI statement can live in addition to the story text (see the AI section below).

### Gaps
- FAQ answers were not captured (the accordions did not expand in the reader), only the questions.
- Two pages are not a frequency sample; no wider tally of multiplayer FAQ questions was made.
- No evidence gathered on refund wording.

## AI disclosure: Kickstarter policy, live wording, backer and press sentiment, harmed campaigns, "No AI" as a selling point, and the most trust-preserving wording for Reef Run

### Takeaway
Reef Run cannot use a "No AI" line or the No Gen AI seal, because its code is AI-assisted and its direct competitor defines the claim to include code ("We do not use AI tools to generate art, design, or code"). The 2026 evidence says the damage comes from disclosure that is late, vague or discovered by others: Stackflow fell from 82% to 42% positive after players found out three months post-launch, and Big Walk was review-bombed over about ten lines; while the most accepted AI use among Steam players is coding help, and 56% of them still want it disclosed.

### Cited Findings

**Kickstarter's rules (Help Center article, undated, "Updated this week" when read on 2026-11-16)** — [Kickstarter Help: AI policy](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/16848396410267):
- Creators answer two yes/no questions: "Does your campaign page contain any generative AI?" and "Will your final project contain any generative AI?"
- If either is yes: "Be specific about which parts of your campaign page or rewards use AI, and how."
- The page then shows one or both set statements: "I am using generative AI content on my campaign page" and "I plan to use generative AI content in my project", followed by the creator's explanation.
- The article does not define generative AI and does not mention code assistants.
- Projects where "AI output makes up most of the work" are not allowed; a project judged to rely too heavily on generative AI "may be declined or, if it has already launched, suspended."
- Original policy announced 2023 (older): disclosures appear in a "Use of AI" section on the live page, in the creator's words, after human review — [Kickstarter, "Introducing Our AI Policy"](https://updates.kickstarter.com/introducing-our-new-ai-policy/) (via search summary).

**Steam's rules** — [Steamworks Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey):
- "Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section." The section covers AI-created content that ships with the game and is consumed by players (artwork, sound, narrative, localization), split into Pre-Generated and Live-Generated.
- Valve rewrote the section around 16–17 January 2026; press reads it as exempting coding helpers — [Notebookcheck, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/) (PC Gamer seen as a search result only).

**Competitor wording — Springfield Hotel**, section headed "No AI in Game Creation" under "Did We Mention..." — [Springfield Hotel campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game):
- "Every part of Springfield Hotel is crafted by people. From the haunting character designs to the intricate tasks, from the environments to the programming itself, everything in-game is created by hands."
- "We do not use AI tools to generate art, design, or code. Every asset you see, every mechanic you play, and every line of writing in this game is the result of human creativity, skill, and passion."
- "We believe games should carry the personality and care of their creators. When you step into Springfield Hotel, you're experiencing something authored by humans, for humans."
- The claim is backed with visible proof next to it: a facial motion-capture test GIF.
- The page shows no "Use of AI" block, consistent with answering "no" to both Kickstarter questions.
- The Boo Croo page has no AI statement of any kind and no "Use of AI" block — [The Boo Croo campaign page](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game).

**Cases where AI-assisted code or late disclosure hurt (2026)**
- Big Walk (House House; launched 4 Aug 2026): the studio's FAQ said chatbot responses were occasionally used as starting points for modified code, estimated at "well under 0.1%" of the code, with no generative AI elsewhere; the main case was about ten lines fixing a Unity URP shadow bug. Steam reviews went from 135 positive / 3 negative on 9 Nov to 426 positive / 366 negative on 10 Nov, then 358 / 90 on 11 Nov. The studio said it had not disclosed on Steam because Valve does not ask about code, apologised, and called its lack of an internal AI policy a failure. The disclosure had been written for its IGF entry after 19 IGF jurors said they would not vote for any game that used generative AI in development — [Dexerto, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/big-walk-review-bombed-after-house-house-admits-ai-touched-well-under-0-1-of-code-3427480/).
- Stackflow (left Early Access July 2026; AI statement 14 Oct 2026): AI used for coding workflow, some sound effects and localization. Positive reviews fell from 82% to 42%. The reviewer's reading is that the timing ("people only found out in October") mattered more than the use itself — [Rogueliker, 21 Oct 2026](https://rogueliker.com/stackflow-late-ai-disclosure-reviews-backlash/).
- Stackflow's store disclosure wording: "Some sound effects and the in-game text localization were created with the help of AI tools during development." / "All AI-generated audio and translated text was reviewed and edited by a human before shipping." / "No AI-generated artwork, and no AI content is generated while the game is running." — [Rogueliker](https://rogueliker.com/stackflow-late-ai-disclosure-reviews-backlash/).
- Postal: Bullet Paradise was cancelled within days of reveal in December 2025 after AI-art accusations the developer denied; the developer studio closed. Not a Kickstarter project — [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2025/12/ai-accusations-killed-a-ps5-ps4-game-in-two-days-and-the-studio-behind-it-is-shutting-down); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/351204/new-postal-game-canceled-one-day-after-reveal-generative-ai-allegations) (both via search summary).
- False accusations also happen: Shrine's Legacy was hit with negative reviews alleging AI; the developer said all work was by hand and posted development code as proof — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/amp/story/gaming/gaming-news/shrines-legacy-devs-deny-ai-slop-chatgpt-rumors) (via search summary).
- Older: Cyan's Kickstarter-funded Firmament drew backer complaints in the Kickstarter comments after "AI assisted content" appeared in its credits (2023) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/firmament-ai-generated-content/); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/myst-devs-used-ai-assistance-to-make-latest-game-and-fans-are-unhappy/1100-6514951/) (via search summary).

**"No AI" as a selling point**
- Broken Sword 2 remaster Kickstarter (2026): headline reports about $400,000 raised after the team promised to drop AI upscaling and hand-draw 55,000 frames, with the line "No AI, just humans" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/adventure/broken-sword-devs-showered-in-usd400-000-after-promising-to-drop-ai-upscaling-and-draw-55-000-frames-by-hand-for-remastered-sequel-no-ai-just-humans/) (headline only retrieved; body not read).
- The "No Gen AI" seal was released free in February 2025 by Polygon Treehouse for store pages "to communicate to potential players that no generative AI was used in its creation" — [GamingOnLinux, Feb 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/02/indie-devs-have-begun-adding-a-no-generative-ai-stamp-to-their-store-pages) (via search summary). Commenters there noted the badge is binary and hard to apply to tools such as machine translation.
- itch.io commenters argue Copilot-generated code counts as AI-generated while editor autocomplete does not — [itch.io forum post](https://itch.io/post/11455510) (community opinion, not policy; via search summary).

**Sentiment data**
- GameDiscoverCo Steam Fan Snapshot, fielded 25 Jun – 2 Jul 2026, about 3,800 self-selected, engaged Steam players — [GameDiscoverCo, 7 Jul 2026](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-do-steam-fans-really-think-about):
  - Games with any AI disclosure: about 43% no big issue, 26% neutral, 31% negative; 8% of all respondents would not buy.
  - 44% read Steam AI disclosures in detail, 45% glance, about 11% ignore them.
  - Only 17% believe developers fully disclose audiovisual AI use.
  - Should AI coding helpers (e.g. Claude Code) be disclosed? 56% yes, 8% no, 37% neutral.
  - Of 2,100 free-text answers, about 51% accept AI for some uses and not others; the most-named acceptable use was coding/programming help (about 239 mentions), then prototyping/placeholder assets (119) and tedious tasks (101).
  - Respondent quotes: "I'm fine with limited AI use for support tasks, not for replacing human creativity." / "I'm OK with AI being used in games as long as there is human review and oversight".
- Game Oracle analysis of about 9,900 Steam games released Jan–Oct 2025: about 21% carried an AI disclosure; after controls, disclosing games had about 53% fewer reviews, and more negative ones. Reviews are a proxy for sales; this is correlation — [PC Gamer, June 2026](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/) (via search summary; one outlet gives a different sample size of 16,554).
- Circana PlayerPulse, December 2025, US: a little over 25% less likely to buy a game using generative AI for art, dialogue, text, music or voice, up from 22% in March 2024 — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/do-gamers-care-about-genai-new-data-reveals-a-silent-majority) (via search summary: **UNVERIFIED** against Circana).
- GDC 2026 State of the Game Industry (about 2,300 industry respondents): 52% say generative AI is having a negative impact on the industry (30% a year earlier, 18% two years earlier); 7% positive; 36% use generative AI tools at work — [GDC](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/) (via search summary); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/111128-more-than-50-game-developers-generative-ai-harming.html).
- Disclosed-AI crowdfunding can still fund: Draconis 8 (tabletop, 2025) raised about $120,000 from just under 1,200 backers with disclosed AI-assisted art and backlash (search summary; original article not identified: **UNVERIFIED**).

### Inferences
- **Disclose, on the page, before launch.** Kickstarter's question is about "generative AI" in the "final project" and does not exempt code. Code produced with an AI coding assistant is generative output that ships in the game, so the conservative honest answer to the second question is yes, with the explanation limited to code. Answering no on the grounds that Steam exempts coding tools is exactly the reasoning House House gave and was punished for. This is my reading of an undefined term; Kickstarter support can confirm.
- **The campaign-page question needs its own honest answer.** If campaign copy was drafted or edited with an AI assistant, the first question ("Does your campaign page contain any generative AI?") is also yes. Either disclose it ("campaign text drafted with an AI writing assistant and edited by us") or have the team rewrite the copy themselves so the answer is truthfully no. The second option is the stronger trust position given the competitor's stance.
- **Do not use "No AI", "made by humans" or the No Gen AI seal.** The seal's stated meaning is no generative AI in creation, and Springfield's version of the claim names code explicitly. A narrower true claim is available: no generative AI art, audio, voices or writing in the game.
- **Lead with what is human-authored and verifiable, then state the AI use plainly, then state control.** This follows Stackflow's three-sentence structure (what used AI / human review / what did not) but delivered up front, which is the part Stackflow got wrong. Naming the exact scope matters: Big Walk's "well under 0.1%" was accurate and still attacked, so the statement will not win over the roughly 8% who refuse any AI; the aim is that nobody can later "discover" anything.
- **Expected cost.** About 31% of engaged Steam players react negatively to any AI disclosure and 8% refuse to buy; but coding is the most-accepted use and 56% want it disclosed. On this evidence an up-front code-only disclosure costs some backers and removes the much larger risk of a post-launch review collapse (Stackflow: 82% to 42%).
- **Asset packs are a second exposure.** "All art from licensed asset packs" is itself something backers may question, and store asset packs can contain AI-generated material. Before writing "no generative AI art", the team should check each pack's listing for an AI declaration. Not verified here.
- **Suggested wording (drafted from the patterns above; not a quoted example).** For the story and the "Use of AI" explanation:

  > **How Reef Run is made, including AI.** Reef Run is built by two people. The game design, the levels, the tuning and every decision about how it plays are ours. The 3D models, textures, sounds and music come from licensed asset packs and from renders we made in the engine; credits are listed in the game. Nothing you see or hear in Reef Run was made with generative AI: no AI art, no AI audio, no AI voices, no AI writing.
  >
  > We do use an AI coding assistant. Part of the game's code was written with it, then read, tested and changed by us. We are telling you now because you should know before you pledge, not find out later. If this matters to you, ask us anything in the comments and we will answer.

  Short form for the Kickstarter field: "Game code was written with the help of an AI coding assistant and reviewed and tested by us. No generative AI is used for art, audio, voices, music or writing. Art and audio are licensed asset packs and our own in-engine renders."
- **Add an FAQ entry** "Did you use AI to make Reef Run?" repeating the same statement, so the story, the Kickstarter "Use of AI" block and the FAQ say the same thing. Use the same wording later on Steam and in the Discord even though Steam does not require it.
- **Be ready to show proof of human work** (Shrine's Legacy posted code; Springfield shows a mocap GIF): development clips, commit history, editor footage.

### Gaps
- No live 2025–2026 Kickstarter video game page with a visible "Use of AI" block disclosing an AI coding assistant was found, so there is no exact precedent wording from Kickstarter; the wording above is drafted, not copied.
- Whether Kickstarter Trust & Safety treats AI coding assistants as "generative AI" under its two questions is not stated anywhere I could read.
- No Kickstarter video game campaign cancelled or suspended specifically over AI use in 2025–2026 was found. The nearest cases are Steam-side (Big Walk, Stackflow) or non-Kickstarter (Postal: Bullet Paradise).
- Springfield Hotel's comment section (24 comments) was not read, so backer reaction to its "No AI" section is unknown.
- Reddit threads (r/kickstarter, r/gamedev, r/IndieDev) were not searched.
- Circana, GDC, Game Oracle, Broken Sword and Draconis 8 figures were seen only through search summaries or headlines.
- One site claims a June 2026 Steam change requiring AI labels on every asset; it conflicts with all other coverage and was not corroborated.

## Backer psychology data 2025–2026: average pledge, price points, early-bird scarcity, demo effect, first-48-hour thresholds

### Takeaway
Hard 2025–2026 data exists for market size (443 funded game campaigns, $26m, $58k average in 2025; about 32% long-run success rate) and for the value of a pre-launch following and a demo. The commonly repeated thresholds ("30% in 48 hours = 85% success", "76% of successful campaigns used early birds") have no traceable dataset and should not be cited as fact.

### Cited Findings
- 2025: 443 successful video game campaigns, about $26m paid out, $58,000 average; 2016 for comparison: 407 campaigns, about $16m, $39,000 average. Totals grew about 7% a year, campaign counts about 1.5% — [GameDiscoverCo, citing Thomas Bidaux (ICO)](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- Long-run (2009–2024) video game success rate 32%; December launches 28%, February 35%; 2024 had 1,335 campaigns raising $25.2m; campaigns with goals under $250K raised 68.1% of all funds — [GameDev Reports, 6 Nov 2025, data by Dmitrii Filatov](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before).
- Demo effect: Prelude Dark Pain raised $157k on a $47k goal, and Bidaux said "a lot of the attention" came from creators with exclusive demo access — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- Pre-launch followers: Autonomica launched with over 10,000 Kickstarter followers and ended with over 12,000 backers; "Coming Soon" pages are increasingly standard — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- Existing audience: Teamcrafter raised €85k by redirecting the audience of the team's Final Fantasy XIV crafting tool; The Witch's Bakery (2024) raised €315k from 7.3k backers, credited to social media presence. Bidaux: paid ads can be ROI-positive but community building is the cornerstone — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- Pledges "sometimes exceed $100 per backer" at the top end; no overall average per backer is published — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding).
- Per-backer averages computed from page figures (my arithmetic): Boo Croo $15,640 / 416 = about $38 (with physical plushie tiers); Space Chef $50,000 / 1,119 = about $45; Springfield Hotel $84,908 / 771 = about $110, which does not match its tier prices and should not be used as a benchmark — [Boo Croo](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game); [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/kickstarter-for-indie-games); [Springfield Hotel](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game).
- Most-backed price points in the two comparable campaigns: $19 at Springfield (380 of 771 backers) and $12 at Boo Croo (182 of 416) — [Springfield rewards](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/msharkie/springfield-hotel-a-social-deduction-horror-game/rewards); [Boo Croo rewards](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/synodic-arc/the-boo-croo-a-chaotic-4-player-party-game/rewards).
- Early-bird scarcity, observed: Springfield's limited $19 tier reached 380 of 385; Boo Croo's $12 early bird reached 182 of 250 and its $22 early bird 50 of 250 — same two rewards pages.
- First 48 hours, one documented case (tabletop, 2024, older and a different category): a first-time creator was 76% funded with 355 backers and $19k at 48 hours and funded a few days later; personal outreach to specific people closed the gap — [Stonemaier Games, 22 Feb 2024](https://stonemaiergames.com/the-first-48-hours-5-takeaways/).
- **UNVERIFIED**: "campaigns that hit 30% of goal in the first 48 hours have an 85% success rate" and "76% of successful campaigns used early-bird rewards" are repeated by several blogs with no dataset or method — surfaced via [TCF blog](https://www.tcf.team/blog/why-crowdfunding-campaigns-succeed-or-fail) and [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/fan-funding-statistics-2026) (search summary only).
- **UNVERIFIED**: a Q1 2026 report lists games at a 99% success rate, which conflicts with the 32% figure above — [TCF Q1 2026 report](https://www.tcf.team/blog/q1-2026-crowdfunding-report) (search summary only).
- Counter-view on early funding: early-bird tiers can inflate early numbers ("once the early bird tiers dried up, so did the pledges") — Stonemaier Games commentary, tabletop, date not confirmed (search summary only: **UNVERIFIED**).

### Inferences
- Reef Run's €14,000 goal sits inside the range small successful game campaigns reach ("$10,000 to $50,000"), and about 40% above what the closest-format campaign (Boo Croo, also a 4-player party game, 30 days) set and achieved ($10k goal, $15.6k raised from 416 backers). At a €15–€20 average digital pledge, €14k needs roughly 700–930 backers, about double Boo Croo's count; the 4-pack is the lever that raises the average without physical goods.
- With digital-only tiers, expect an average pledge well under Boo Croo's $38 unless packs sell.
- The best-supported levers are a pre-launch follower list and a playable demo in creators' hands, not early-bird mechanics.
- Launch month: a 30-day campaign started in December would fall in the historically weakest month (28% vs 35% in February).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 dataset on average pledge per backer for digital-only PC games.
- No sourced first-48-hour threshold; no Kicktraq daily curves were pulled for Springfield Hotel or The Boo Croo, so their day-1/day-2 share is unknown.
- No controlled evidence on early-bird scarcity; only the two observed fill rates.
- BackerKit, Launchboom, Full Charge and Crowdfunding Nerds material was not retrieved.
